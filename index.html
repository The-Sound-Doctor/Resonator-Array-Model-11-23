What is plotted? Both graphs correspond to pressure at the current microphone position, relative to the same source drive. Graph A calculates the empty tube with all side branches disconnected. Graph B calculates the same tube with all enabled resonator side branches connected. Choose Tube frequency response to plot transfer level 20 log₁₀(|pmic/pdrive|) for a separate 1 Pa RMS test tone at each frequency. Choose Source-driven mic spectrum for the predicted actual spectrum from the selected excitation: dB SPL per 1 Hz bin for noise / illustrative sweep occupancy, or a single RMS tone for sine. A linear system's transfer function itself does not depend on whether the drive is white noise, pink noise, or a sine wave. Move your cursor over either chart to compare the two configurations and read their difference.

Physics: Tube segments use 2-port acoustic transfer matrices in pressure / volume flow coordinates. Each side branch is a shunt admittance. A Helmholtz branch uses Z = R + jωρLeff/S − jρc²/(ωV) with a nominal circular-neck end correction. A quarter-wave branch uses Zin = Zc coth[(α+jk)L] + Zentry. Viscothermal boundary-layer losses are estimated using smooth circular passages. The source has adjustable finite acoustic source impedance. The outlet load provides reflections and, for the open case, approximate radiation outside the pipe.

Shape fidelity: Straight, J and spiral change the packaging sketch and can use different measured centerline lengths. With equal cross-section, path length and ideal smooth-wall losses, the 1D responses coincide; no arbitrary bend multipliers are introduced. Bend scattering, detailed 3D ports, nonlinear jetting, wall flexure, microphone obstruction, higher modes, and accurate near-field outlet radiation are not resolved.

What does the acoustic solver actually compute?

With harmonic convention ejωt, each tube section of length ℓ has a transfer matrix [[cosh(γℓ), Zcsinh(γℓ)], [sinh(γℓ)/Zc, cosh(γℓ)]]. Each branch has transfer matrix [[1,0], [1/Zbranch,1]]. The product relates outlet (pL,QL) to inlet (p0,Q0), solved with pL=ZLQL and pdrive=p0+ZsourceQ0. For a blocked outlet, the rigid termination is represented by QL=0 to avoid numerical infinities. At in-tube microphone positions the downstream matrix predicts local pressure; outside an open outlet a monopole far-field proxy predicts pressure from QL. The loss coefficient for smooth tubes uses the classical high-frequency viscothermal boundary-layer approximation; Helmholtz neck losses interpolate by taking the larger of laminar and boundary-layer resistance. All approximations are flagged in the limits panel.

Sources, validity and physical assumptions
  1. Piana et al. (2022), Silencer Design for the Control of Low Frequency Noise in Ventilation Ducts — straight sections, side-branch T matrices, source/load coupling, reference vs installed insertion loss.
  2. Ingard (1953), On the Theory and Design of Acoustic Resonators — cavity neck mass, end corrections, viscous and thermal losses.
  3. Stinson (1991), The propagation of plane sound waves in narrow and wide circular tubes — thermoviscous propagation theory.
  4. Catapane et al. (2023), Coiled quarter wavelength resonators... — comparison of coiled and straight quarter-wave pathways, geometric and dissipative effects.
  5. Guo et al. (2020), A compact low-frequency sound-absorbing metasurface constructed by resonator with embedded spiral neck — long spiral neck Helmholtz devices.
  6. ISO 10534-2:2023 — actual impedance-tube practice, plane-wave limits and microphone arrangements (standard, not a paper).

This simulator uses its own demonstration geometry, not dimensions copied from any one fabricated specimen in those publications. Change dimensions to match the paper or your prototype.

A useful build strategy: design for predicted attenuation first, measure tube transmission with one or two calibrated microphones, estimate neck/bend losses from the measurement, then use FEM/BEM for three-dimensional geometry before fabrication. This software is educational/research prototyping, not a validated measurement instrument. A bank of tuned side branches is not automatically a homogenized effective-medium metamaterial.
